Read this first

Four structural changes that explain almost everything

Nearly every question a client or article raises about the new Act traces back to one of these four moves.

819 → 536 sections

Provisos and explanations were folded into the main text, tables and formulas replaced dense prose, and obsolete provisions were dropped. Same policy, leaner code.

23 chapters · 16 schedules

One year concept: “Tax Year”

“Previous Year” and “Assessment Year” are both gone. A Tax Year runs 1 April–31 March; income is assessed after it ends — exactly as before. Only the naming changed.

Sec. 3

TDS collapsed into a few sections

40-plus scattered TDS provisions now sit in two parent sections with payment codes. Salary stands alone; everything else is tabular. Rates and thresholds are untouched.

Sec. 392 · 393 · 394

Section 10 → Schedules

The catch-all home of exempt incomes leaves the main body: Sec. 10 becomes Sec. 11 read with Schedules II–VII. HRA sits in a Schedule entry; gratuity, commuted pension and leave encashment fold into Sec. 19 with the old Sec. 16.

Day-one reference

Sec. 393 payment codes

From 1 April 2026, challans and quarterly returns identify a payment by a four-digit code, not by “194C” or “194J”. Quoting an old section number on a new-Act return makes it defective. Search by code, by the old section, or by keyword.

The overlap you're living in right now

What moves to 2025 — and what stays under 1961

Through this year you'll work in both Acts at once. The cut-off is clean: it turns on when the income was earned, not when you file.

Governed by the 2025 Act

  • Income earned from 1 April 2026 — Tax Year 2026-27 onwards.
  • All new TDS/TCS on payments made on or after 1 April 2026 — use the new section codes on challans.
  • Fresh return forms, the Tax Year label, and Rules 2026 forms (e.g. the Form 26AS successor).

Stays under the 1961 Act

  • FY 2025-26 returns (AY 2026-27) — filed under the old Act even though you file them after April 2026.
  • Pending assessments, appeals and proceedings for pre-April-2026 periods — old timelines and rules continue.
  • Old circulars, notifications and instructions — valid unless they clash with the new Act (saved by Sec. 536).

Quick answers

Questions clients and juniors keep asking

Do FY 2025-26 returns go under the old Act or the new one?
The old 1961 Act. FY 2025-26 (AY 2026-27) is filed under the 1961 Act even though the filing happens after 1 April 2026. The 2025 Act applies to income from 1 April 2026 — Tax Year 2026-27 — so its first returns are filed in the 2027 cycle.
Have tax slabs or rates changed?
No. Slabs and rates under both the old and new regimes are unchanged. The new regime stays the default with income up to ₹12 lakh effectively tax-free and a ₹75,000 standard deduction; the old regime remains available. This is a drafting and structure reform, not a rate change.
Where did Section 80C go?
It is now Sec. 123, read with Schedule XV. The ₹1.5 lakh ceiling and the eligible investments (LIC, PPF, ELSS, tuition fees, housing-loan principal) are unchanged, and it remains an old-regime-only deduction. Old Secs. 80CCC and 80CCE fold into the same section; NPS under 80CCD becomes Sec. 124.
Was Section 194C removed?
Not in substance. Contractor-payment TDS is now inside Sec. 393 with the appropriate payment code. When filing TDS returns for payments on or after 1 April 2026, reference Sec. 393 and the code rather than Sec. 194C. The 1% rate and thresholds are unchanged.
Is “Assessment Year” gone completely?
For periods from 1 April 2026, yes — use Tax Year. Both “Previous Year” and “Assessment Year” are replaced by one 12-month Tax Year. The old terms still apply to pre-April-2026 periods and their pending proceedings.
